Histosentience — a three-day artist-run programme of performance, 8–10 December 2026, au JUS and Metarage, Saint-Gilles, Brussels

“The trees, the rocks, the animals, they are not [histosentient]. But your laugh, that is a histosentient sound. The word means historically sentient; and that means knowing where you've been, where you are, and where you're going. It also means appreciating it.” Out of the Dead City by Samuel R. Delany

Histosentience is an artist-run programme of performance, bringing together three artistic works presented over three consecutive days, each followed by a conversation with the artist. Taking on the concept of histosentience from Samuel R. Delany’s novel Out of the Dead City (1968), which describes a particular form of intelligence that is conscious of its own historicity, the programme proposes a framework in which the works become contexts for one another, allowing their forms, questions and methods to be read relationally, while the artists themselves continue to shape the discourse surrounding their work.

Histosentience je program izvedbi koji vode umjetnici, a okuplja tri umjetnička rada predstavljena tijekom tri uzastopna dana, nakon kojih slijedi razgovor s umjetnicima. Free entry upon reservation, donation at the door (suggested 10 EUR per performance or 25 EUR for all three). Each performance is followed by a reflection session — an after talk amongst the showing artists.

  • 2 0 0 9 — Stefa Govaart & Marko Gutić Mižimakov

    08 DEC ’26, 16h first performance, 19h second performance, au JUS, Saint-Gilles

    is a performance in an exhibition setting that reflects on depiction, technology and historicity, and uses argument—the practice of persuasion—as a performative device.

    Two film characters, Печал and Kälte, dialoguing through walkie talkies, meet in a world disclosing itself in fragments including a Still Life, Nameless Cityscape, and Memory Ribbons. Before physically sharing weight, they communicate a set of coordinates that reflect on coming into political consciousness.

    The performance is organized around this encounter with oddly grouped items. Clustered portraits of public figures printed on canvas and cut into ribbons, reconstructed from memory, stand alongside a still life composed of objects refracted through a mirrored sphere. Over the course of the performance, the gathered objects amass referentiality underlining social relations of power and reproduction. The Nameless Cityscape displays an appropriated Aeropittura style painting. Speed and altitude break down into dream-like forms of “the future”. Fetishizing bird’s-eye view, seeking to represent representation as such, for the Italian futurists, Aeropittura was to impress its viewers: look what technology can do! 2 0 0 9 borrows this disputed backdrop for a final call to action.

    Ripped out of the calendar, for us, the title points to the long first decade of the twenty-first century, a memorable epoch, a coming-of-age story of sorts, today distant enough to return as a problem and demand interpretation. Looking back, we ask how we became available to the popular forms that now organize us.

    Marko Gutić Mižimakov is a visual, performance and text-based artist living between Brussels and Zagreb. Through intimate and collaborative processes, they animate and choreograph bodies and objects, digital and palpable, into non-orientable forms. Their work increasingly deals with matter as stored time, the sediment of social relations and the frictions that shift them. Current projects include 2 0 0 9 and Sagittarius A* with Stefa Govaart, and Govaart’s film Duplicity. They are an external associate at the Academy of Fine Arts in Zagreb, where they also co-organise the artist-run festival PoslijeSvijet (AfterWorld). In Brussels, they are currently organising Histosentience, a performance programme hosted by au JUS and Metarage.

    Stefa Govaart is a Brussels-based performance artist. Collaborative in nature, their work probes the stakes of critique today, and considers the performer as “function” caught up in distinct forms of institutionality. They are currently collaborating with Marko Gutić Mižimakov on two film projects and the performance titled 2 0 0 9. Govaart performed in works by Oscar Murillo/Lydia McGlinchey, Eszter Salamon, Claudia Pagès Rabal, among others. BEGIN/The Mirror (2024) is a dance performance for the theater made in collaboration with Bryana Fritz and Chloe Chignell (Beursschouwburg, Brussels, October 30-31). They teach writing and coordinate the theory programme at P.A.R.T.S. in Brussels.

  • Sun Cut: a lover’s dictionary — Chloe Chignell

    09 DEC ’26, 15h doors open, 15h30 performance, Metarage, Saint-Gilles

    is a living lexicon of 366 gestures that embodies the 366 entries in Lesbian Peoples: Material for a Dictionary written by Monique Wittig and Sandie Zeig (1979). In an act of speculative reanimation Sun Cut is an anticipatory document remembering a future yet to arrive. The work unfurls word by word, body by body. Each iteration of the performance progresses through the dictionary: gathering bodies, gestures, dances. It resists linguistic fixity in order to embrace language as a site of transformation, refusal, and reinvention. Inverting the status of printed material in archival practices Sun Cut asks how the body can be both a site and means of preservation.

    Lesbian Peoples: Material for a Dictionary is a speculative dictionary that dismantles patriarchal language to posit a world of lesbian relation. Through fragmented definitions and poetic inventions, it reframes language as a site of resistance. Its entries range from definitions of everyday terms to elaborate accounts of mythical events, political movements and cultural practices. The book itself is a queer critique of linguistic authority and its role in enforcing dominant ideologies. Wittig and Zeig subvert the dictionary’s normal function, as a tool of mastery and control, and reshape it into a generative field where language destabilises its own conditions of use. Building on these foundations, Sun Cut approaches Lesbian Peoples not simply as a book but as a practice—a framework for reorganising the body through language.

    Chloe Chignell is an artist working across text, choreography and publishing. Her work focuses on language within a choreographic frame. She invests in writing as a body building practice and in practices of experimental translation. In 2019, Chloe co-founded rile* a bookshop and project space for publication and performance with Sven Dehens. Her work has been presented across Europe and Australia including Beursschouwburg Brussels, Dancehouse Melbourne, NEXT Festival, Pavillon ADC Geneva, Batard Festival, Saal Biennale, Moving Words Norway, KAAP Bruges, Littérature etc. Paris, and Venice Biennale of Dance.

    Steph Holl-Trieu is an artist and writer. Her work explores social relationships that shape modes of perception, the production of meaning and experience – both abstract and concrete. Through text, installation, sound, performative readings and role-play, she develops structures that deconstruct and reconfigure existing forms of meaning-making. She regards aesthetic experience as indeterminate and unruly; her works emerge through a process-oriented approach and frequently within collaborative or collective contexts. Her works have been presented at venues including the Shedhalle, Zurich, CH; HAU Berlin, DE; Kunstraum Niederösterreich, Vienna, AT; and Le Commun, Geneva, CH. Her art-critical texts have appeared in publications such as Texte zur Kunst and PW-Magazine.

  • Dolls & Goats — Zrinka Užbinec

    10 DEC ’26, 18h doors open, 19h performance, au JUS, Saint-Gilles

    From 18:00 the space is open for reading, together or alone, with or without conversation, with books scattered around. (Turn a page, write down the memory of a movement, fossilise a sensation.) At 19:00 the doors close and the performance begins.

    The performance could start with a doll-like body. A doll-like body could lie naked on the lawn, float in the river, stare from the car. She could be frozen in the fridge, covered in plastic on the riverbank, lying in bed as if nothing had happened. A doll-like body could be missing; a day, missing for two, for a week, 96 hours with a hope to reappear, placed perfectly, washed neatly, always in the same pose, waiting to be found, seen on screen, avenged. It will probably start with a doll-unlike body sitting still.

    (Dolls and Goats is a choreographic solo which explodes Coppélia, exploring the range between cuteness and violence. It has changed its skin more than once. Its choreography has been skinned, tanned and then kept in a book. It is still danced.)

    Zrinka Užbinec (they/she) is a choreographer, dance artist and researcher. They hold a practice-led PhD in choreography from the Centre for Dance Research (C-DaRE), Coventry University, and an MA in Choreography and Performance from JLU Giessen. They were a long-standing member of the collective BADco. (2006–2020) and today work as an author and performer in Croatia and internationally. Their work explores choreography as a method, the relationship between dance, text, objects and drawing, and the metastability of performative and bodily states.
